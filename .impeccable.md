# Design Context

## Design Context

### Users

Developers and technical evaluators discovering `xmeta` from GitHub,
Hacker News, or a SQLite/CLI tooling list. They care about how it works, whether
it is safe, and how quickly they can try it. They are comfortable with terminals,
SQL, and extended attributes, and they are skeptical of marketing language.

### Brand Personality

Calm, clean, quietly capable. A light technical tool that gets out of the way.
The voice is concise and factual; the interface shows the product through
queries, file rows, and commands instead of adjectives. The emotional goal is
clarity: "I understand this, and I can try it now."

### Aesthetic Direction

Match the official eidos.space visual system: warm paper (#f8f7f3), warm ink
(#26241e), soft rules (#e4e1d7), and a sparse violet accent (#4a3fa3). Use IBM
Plex Serif for editorial headings, IBM Plex Sans for body and navigation, and
IBM Plex Mono for commands and technical labels. Keep the centered hero,
compact navigation, restrained 4–6px corners, dark warm terminal panels, and
generous whitespace. Use color sparingly for focus, active tabs, and data states.

Anti-references: heavy cobalt poster blocks, brutalist borders, dark neon
developer tools, purple/cyan gradients, glassmorphism, floating card grids,
oversized icon tiles, generic SaaS hero metrics.

### Design Principles

1. **Show the database, not a promise.** Lead with real SQL, result rows, and
   terminal commands.
2. **Air is structure.** Use whitespace, thin rules, and scale to organize the
   page instead of boxes and shadows.
3. **Stay in the Eidos family.** Reuse the official site's typography and paper
   palette. Use violet to identify interaction, not as a decorative background.
4. **Light but legible.** Keep the palette restrained without sacrificing contrast,
   focus states, or readable code.
5. **Immediate content.** Keep content visible from first paint. Use small
   interaction feedback, respect reduced motion, and keep content usable without
   JavaScript.
